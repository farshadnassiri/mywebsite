Questions owners ask about the review

What do I actually receive at the end?

A written report — typically 12 to 20 pages — with a section per area, the figures behind each finding, and the schedules used to produce them. Then a one-page plan ranking what to do over 30, 60 and 90 days. Plus a call to go through it and argue with any of it.

How much of my time does it take?

About ninety minutes in total: a 30-minute scoping call at the start, a short data request you forward to whoever keeps your books, and a 60-minute walkthrough at the end. The analysis in between does not need you.

Our records are a mess. Should we tidy them up first?

No — and waiting for tidy records is how a year goes by. Incomplete books are the normal starting point. If the underlying records exist, the analysis can be rebuilt from them, and that rebuilding is often the first thing of value you get.

We are profitable already. Is there anything in it for us?

Usually more than for a struggling business. Healthy overall margins are exactly what hides the specific clients, products and jobs that lose money — the aggregate covers for them. The reviews that find the most are rarely the ones that felt urgent.

What if you find nothing?

Then the report says so in writing, and you have an independent second opinion on your finances. That is a legitimate result. I would rather write that report than manufacture findings to justify the fee.

Is this going to turn into a sales process?

The plan will name things that need doing. Some I could do; some your existing accountant should handle; some you should do yourselves and some need nobody. If the honest recommendation is that you do not need me further, it will say that. There is no follow-up sequence.

Before you send a stranger your numbers

Working with your financial information, safely.

No banking access. Ever.

I never ask for bank logins or the ability to move money. Read-only reports from whatever system you already use are enough.

Anonymise before you send

Customer and employee names can be replaced with codes first. The analysis needs the amounts and the structure, not the identities.

NDA first, if you prefer

Happy to sign yours before the first call. If you do not have one, I will send a short mutual NDA. Client work is confidential either way.

“We don’t have those reports”

Almost nobody does. If the underlying records exist — invoices, ledgers, payroll, bank statements — the analysis can be rebuilt from them.

Whatever system you use

QuickBooks, Xero, Zoho, Sage, an ERP, or spreadsheets. I work from the reports it can produce, so nothing needs to change on your side.

Clear professional boundaries

Advisory and internal audit work — not statutory audit, external assurance, legal or tax advice. Said plainly so nobody is misled. Scope
