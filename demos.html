Worked examples

Four questions, and the analysis that answers each one.

These are not product demonstrations and the deliverable is not a screen. Each one shows how a specific owner question gets turned into figures you can act on — the same sequence a real engagement follows, on a constructed example business so the numbers can be shown openly.

Money and margin

Is the work we do actually profitable?

Profitability01

Which clients make money, and which quietly do not

Ten clients, one overhead pool, three ways of sharing that overhead out. Switch between them and the ranking rearranges — which is why the basis has to be agreed before anyone acts on the numbers. Ends on the question every owner eventually asks: should we drop this client?

Answers: Am I profitable? · Which work should we stop? · What does a client really cost to serve?

Pricing02

What a price change actually does to profit

The gap between what a job appears to cost and what it really costs, then what happens when the price moves. Includes the number owners consistently get wrong: how much volume you could afford to lose before an increase stops being worth it.

Answers: Are we charging enough? · What if I raise prices 10%? · Should I accept this discount?

Cash and commitments

Will the money be there when it is needed?

Cash03

Thirteen weeks of cash, and the week that breaks

A weekly picture driven by growth, how long customers take to pay, payroll timing and the quarterly bills nobody diarises. Shows the trough week, the shortfall against your own safety floor, and — separately — what hiring two people does to it.

Answers: How long does our cash last? · Can we afford to hire? · Why is there less cash when we are growing?

See the analysis
Controls04

Money leaving without anyone questioning it

A month of payments tested end to end: paid twice, split just under an approval limit, entered and approved by one person, posted at odd hours, or sent to a supplier set up days earlier. Every transaction, not a sample of twenty.

Answers: Could we be paying twice? · Are our approval limits working? · Is anyone checking?

What these are, and what they are not

Each page shows roughly the first fifth of a real engagement — the part that can be published, because the business behind it is invented. What is missing is everything specific to you: your cost structure, your contracts, your payment terms, the reasons your margins look the way they do, and the judgement about what to actually do next. That part is the engagement. Every page says plainly which items it leaves out.

Figures come from a constructed example business, not a client. As clients agree to be referenced, anonymised real outcomes will sit alongside these.
