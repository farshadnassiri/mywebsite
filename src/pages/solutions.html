Nobody arrives knowing they need “a costing review”. They arrive with a question they cannot answer from their accounting system. This is that list — organised the way the problem shows up, rather than the way a firm sells it.

Cash “Where is my money actually going?” Profit says one thing, the bank balance says another. The gap is almost always working capital: receivables, inventory, prepayments and the timing of large fixed payments. See the analysisForecasting
Cash “How long do we have before this becomes a problem?” Runway measured weekly, not as a single number. The question that matters is which specific week breaks, and what would have to be true to avoid it. See the analysisForecasting
Cash “Customers pay us late. How much is it costing?” Days-sales-outstanding converted into money, plus where the delay actually starts — usually invoicing practice and terms, not the customer. Working capital reviewForecasting
Profitability “Are we actually profitable?” At company level, per client, per product, per job. Aggregate profit routinely hides a portfolio where a third of the work loses money. See the analysisCosting
Profitability “Which clients or products should we stop serving?” A question with a counterintuitive answer, because allocated overhead does not leave when the client does. Usually the fix is repricing, not resignation. Test dropping a clientCosting
Profitability “What does one unit of our work really cost?” Direct cost is the easy half. The hard half is the overhead nobody allocates and the time nobody records — which is where the margin quietly goes. Costing reviewCosting
Pricing “Are we charging enough?” Almost always answerable, and almost never answered, because the true cost per unit has never been established. Once it is, the price question resolves itself quickly. See the analysisPricing
Pricing “What happens if I raise prices 10%?” The useful version of the question: how much volume could you afford to lose before the increase stops paying for itself? That number is usually far larger than owners expect. See the analysisPricing
Pricing “Our biggest customer wants a discount.” Volume-for-price trades modelled properly, including the contribution you must replace and the precedent it sets for the rest of the book. Pricing reviewPricing
Growth & hiring “Can we afford to hire?” A hire is a multi-year cash commitment decided in an afternoon. Modelled against the cash floor, not the P&L, with a start date you can actually defend. See the analysisForecasting
Growth & hiring “We are growing but there is less cash than last year.” The classic profitable-but-broke pattern. Growth funded by receivables consumes cash before it produces any — and it accelerates as you succeed. See the analysisForecasting
Growth & hiring “Should we take on this large contract?” Margin, cash profile and concentration risk together. A contract can be profitable and still be the thing that breaks the business. Decision supportFractional CFO
Forecasting “What does next year look like?” A budget built from drivers you control — volume, price, headcount, terms — rather than last year plus ten percent. Budget & forecastForecasting
Forecasting “What if our best month never comes?” Base, downside and upside built on the same model, so the difference between them is assumptions rather than mood. Then: what would we do first in each case. Scenario planningForecasting
Forecasting “The bank wants projections.” Lender-ready forecasts with the workings visible and the assumptions defensible — the part that decides whether the conversation goes well. Forecast packForecasting
Risk & controls “Could money leave without anyone noticing?” Duplicate payments, invoices split under approval limits, one person entering and approving, payments to newly created vendors. Testable on every transaction. See the analysisControls
Risk & controls “We have grown; our processes have not.” Approval limits, segregation of duties, system access and the month-end close, reviewed against the size the business is now — not the size it was. Controls reviewControls
Risk & controls “Can someone independent look at how this works?” A remote internal audit of a specific process — procurement, payroll, revenue, expenses — with findings, ratings and an owner-assigned action plan. Internal auditControls
Reporting “Our reports arrive too late to be useful.” A close calendar and a reporting pack that lands within days of month-end, containing the six numbers that change behaviour rather than forty that do not. Management reportingReporting
Reporting “We rebuild the same spreadsheet every month.” Scattered records brought into one reconciled view that is produced the same way every month, instead of rebuilt from scratch each time. Management reportingReporting
Reporting “We don’t have the reports you are asking for.” Nobody does, at first. If the raw data exists — invoices, ledger exports, payroll, bank statements — cash flow, AR ageing and costing can be reconstructed from it. How that worksReporting
