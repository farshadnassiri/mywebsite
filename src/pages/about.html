About

An accountant who builds the tools, not a developer who read a finance book.

The order matters. The financial judgement comes first — what a cost really is, how it should be allocated, which control actually reduces risk, whether a forecast is defensible. The technology exists to deliver that judgement faster and to more businesses than a traditional practice can reach.

Background

My working life has been in accounting and finance — the practical end of it: ledgers that did not reconcile, month-ends that ran late, costing that nobody had ever tested, and owners making significant decisions on numbers they did not fully trust. That is the environment this practice is built for, because it is the one most small businesses are actually in.

Alongside that, I am an ACCA candidate, working through the qualification that underpins the technical side — financial reporting, management accounting, performance management, audit and assurance, financial management. It matters here because the difference between an interesting chart and a defensible number is exactly what that syllabus is about.

Why the technical side exists

I write code, build models, automate reporting and use AI as a working tool. None of that is the product. It is the reason a small business can get analysis that would normally require a finance team: reconstructing a cash flow statement from raw exports, testing an entire payment population instead of a sample of twenty, or turning a pricing question into a model the owner can interrogate themselves.

A traditional practice would charge for the hours that work takes. Automating it is how the same analysis becomes affordable at this size — and why the deliverable tends to be an instrument you keep rather than a document you file.

One outcome, several skills

Accounting, financial analysis, programming and AI look like four different careers on a CV. They are one job here: helping an owner make a better financial decision, with less delay and less guesswork. Everything on this site — the services, the models, the reviews — points at that single outcome. If something would not improve a decision, it does not get built.

Working together remotely

How your data is handled

Sending financial data to someone you have not met is a reasonable thing to hesitate over. Here is the standing position, before you ask.

No credentials, no payment access

I never ask for bank logins, card details or payment authority, and I do not need access to your accounting system. Read-only exports are enough for everything described on this site.

Anonymise first if you want

Customer, employee and supplier names can be replaced with codes before anything is sent. The analysis needs structure and amounts, not identities.

NDA on request

I will sign yours before the first call. If you do not have one, I will send a short mutual NDA. Client work is confidential by default either way.

Least data necessary

The data request is specific and minimal rather than “send everything”. If a file is not needed for a finding, it is not requested.

Any accounting system

QuickBooks, Xero, Zoho, Sage, MYOB, an ERP, or spreadsheets. I work from exports, so your software choice is not a constraint and no migration is involved.

Incomplete records are normal

Missing cash flow statements, no AR ageing, no costing analysis — this is the usual starting point. If the raw data exists, the reports can be reconstructed from it.

Scope

What I do — and what I do not.

Within scope

  • Management accounting and financial analysis
  • FP&A, budgeting, forecasting and scenario planning
  • Costing, pricing and margin analysis
  • Internal controls review and internal audit as a management service
  • Finance automation, reporting and dashboards
  • Fractional CFO advisory and decision support
  • Accounting and reporting support, cleanup and reconciliation

Outside scope

  • Statutory or external audit. Not offered, and not implied by the internal-audit service. That requires a licensed audit firm in your jurisdiction.
  • Assurance engagements and formal opinions on financial statements.
  • Tax advice, filings and representation. Referred to a licensed tax professional.
  • Legal advice of any kind, including contract and regulatory opinions.
  • Regulated financial or investment advice.

This distinction is stated everywhere it could be misread, on purpose. “Internal audit” and “audit” are different services with different legal meanings, and a client who does not understand the difference has been sold something they did not agree to. Where you need a statutory audit, a tax opinion or a legal position, I will refer you rather than improvise.

How I work

Fixed fees, scoped in writing

No hourly billing and no open-ended engagements. You approve a written scope and a fixed price before anything starts. If the scope changes, we agree a new price before the work happens, not after.

Findings are quantified or they are not findings

Every conclusion carries a number: cash impact, margin impact, or risk exposure. “This could be better” is not a deliverable. If I cannot attach an amount to something, I will say that too.

You keep the models

The spreadsheets, models and dashboards built during an engagement are yours, with the workings visible. I am not interested in being a dependency you cannot leave.

Honest about limits

The demonstrations on this site use synthetic data and say so on every page. The client list is short because the practice is new. Where something is outside my scope or my competence, the answer is a referral. None of that is a weakness worth hiding — it is the basis on which a finance relationship has to start.
